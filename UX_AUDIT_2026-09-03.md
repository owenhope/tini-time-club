### Product hierarchy to review

The five primary destinations are Feed, Explore, Review, Index, and Profile. Explore then contains three peer destinations (Map, Golden Glass, Members), while Activity and Settings sit behind header actions. This is structurally sound, but it creates three terminology questions:

1. The landing CTA says “Discover Martinis,” the tab says “Explore,” and the marketing capture says “Discover.” Pick one top-level verb.
2. The tab label “Index” is less clear than the in-product name “Martini Index.” If space forces “Index,” onboarding should teach the term.
3. “Review” is used as a tab action while the app’s domain language also treats a review as content. “Log” is the clearer verb and already appears in accessibility copy.

## Findings

### P0 — fixed on this branch

#### 1. The welcome screen asks users to agree to documents they cannot open

The legal statement is plain text, even though it names “Terms and Privacy Policy.” A visitor cannot inspect either document before continuing. Settings exposes only Terms of Service, and no standalone privacy route exists.

Impact: broken trust at the highest-stakes acquisition moment, accessibility failure for link discovery, and avoidable legal/compliance risk.

Recommendation: make Terms and Privacy Policy separate links on Welcome; expose both in Settings; use one canonical content source rather than duplicating prose in onboarding and the Terms screen.

Evidence: `app/welcome.tsx:125`, `app/(tabs)/(profile)/settings.tsx:79`, `app/(tabs)/(profile)/terms.tsx:5`, `app/onboarding.tsx:704`.

### P1 — high-impact UX issues addressed on this branch

#### 2. Explore’s header has too much navigation packed into one band

The screen combines title, search, region selection, and a three-way segmented control. The map capture shows four distinct choices before any map interaction. The 38×38 search target is below the app’s own 44-point control convention, and the icon is only 18 points.

Impact: scanning cost, missed taps, and a particularly cramped experience with large text or long translated region names.

Recommendation: make search a full-width field or a 44-point control; keep region selection on a second row; test 200% Dynamic Type and long region names.

Evidence: `components/explore/ExploreScreen.tsx:99`, `components/explore/ExploreScreen.tsx:199`.

#### 3. Notification toggles fail silently and can display the wrong final state

The reminder path restores its optimistic override when persistence fails, but gives no error message. The mentions path ignores `updateProfile` errors entirely and clears its override, so a failed switch can appear to snap back with no explanation.

Impact: users cannot tell whether an important preference was saved.

Recommendation: disable the row while saving, retain or roll back deterministically, and show inline retry/error copy.

Evidence: `app/(tabs)/(profile)/notifications.tsx:27`.

#### 4. The feed card is visually overloaded

The current capture puts identity/rank, overflow, two tags, venue metadata, two component ratings, overall rating, engagement actions, caption/comments, and relative time into one card. The strongest content—the photograph and verdict—competes with metadata, while repeated dividers and dark containers flatten hierarchy.

Impact: slower feed scanning and less obvious primary actions.

Recommendation: keep identity, image, place, overall verdict, and engagement in the default state; move detailed taste/presentation breakdown and comment preview behind an expansion or detail view. Limit the image overlay to the place name plus one supporting line.

Evidence: `components/ReviewItem.tsx` (1,133 lines) and `app-store-designs/feed-1242x2688.png`.

#### 5. The profile header tries to present every profile concept at once

The capture simultaneously shows display name twice, avatar/rank, three stat cards, rank badge, bio, favorite venue, spirit/type tags, and the Reviews/Regulars switch before content begins. The duplicated “Owen” is the clearest symptom.

Impact: weak hierarchy and excessive vertical cost before the user’s actual history.

Recommendation: show one name treatment, avatar, concise bio, and tappable stats first. Move preferences and favorite venue into a compact “Taste profile” section below the tabs or collapse them into one summary row.

Evidence: `components/ProfileHeader.tsx`, `components/profile/ProfileBody.tsx`, `app-store-designs/profile-1242x2688.png`.

#### 6. Golden Glass looks authoritative without explaining its ranking contract

The list says “The best martinis” and “places the club is raising a glass to right now,” but the visible interface does not explain minimum review count, time window, ties, or ranking method. Cards with only three or four reviews can appear definitive.

Impact: users may distrust rankings or misunderstand a promotional-looking feature.

Recommendation: add a concise “How it works” disclosure beside the heading and show qualification metadata when sample sizes are small.

Evidence: `components/explore/GoldenGlassList.tsx`, `app-store-designs/golden-glass-1242x2688.png`.

### P2 — consistency and maintainability issues

#### 7. Four core experiences are too large to preserve consistent UX safely

`app/review.tsx` is 1,412 lines, `app/(tabs)/(home)/home.tsx` is 1,256, `components/ReviewItem.tsx` is 1,133, and `components/nav/AppHeader.tsx` is 1,024. They mix orchestration, mutations, navigation, formatting, animation, and styling.

Impact: small spacing or state changes have a wide regression surface; equivalent states are likely to drift.

Recommendation: deepen modules around the review composer flow, feed controller, review-card sections, and header variants. Keep route modules as coordinators and make shared visual states testable through small interfaces.

#### 8. Profile and review routes bypass the intended data seam

The architecture says routes should coordinate and services should own data behavior. The profile route directly uploads/removes storage objects and updates review rows; the review route directly coordinates multiple option/mention/publishing concerns.

Impact: error handling and loading behavior are harder to reuse or test, and user feedback becomes inconsistent.

Recommendation: move avatar mutation and review deletion behind service interfaces; keep the route responsible for intent and rendering only.

Evidence: `app/(tabs)/(profile)/profile.tsx:135`, `app/review.tsx:279`, `architecture.md`.

#### 9. Spacing still mixes tokens with one-off numbers

Examples include 5/10px form margins, a 14px review gutter, 38px controls, and a 148px celebration offset. These values may be intentional individually, but together they create a second informal spacing system alongside theme tokens.

Impact: uneven rhythm and extra tuning work across devices and text sizes.

Recommendation: add explicit compact/control/overlay tokens where current tokens do not fit, then remove raw layout numbers except for true geometry or hairlines.

Evidence: `components/SelectableOptionsInput.tsx:79`, `components/MultiSelectInput.tsx:73`, `app/review.tsx:1261`, `components/CelebrationModal.tsx:158`.

#### 10. Similar selection controls have different visual and behavioral contracts

The shared `SegmentedControl`, Settings appearance selector, `SelectableOptionsInput`, and `MultiSelectInput` all represent selection but independently define padding, selected colors, press feedback, and accessibility roles. Single-select options use `button` rather than `radio` in one flow.

Impact: inconsistent interaction learning and repeated styling code.

Recommendation: keep distinct layouts, but share one selection-control state model and primitives for selected/pressed/disabled/focus behavior.

Evidence: `components/shared/SegmentedControl.tsx`, `app/(tabs)/(profile)/settings.tsx:127`, `components/SelectableOptionsInput.tsx`, `components/MultiSelectInput.tsx`.

#### 11. Screenshot/test scaffolding leaks through production modules

`screenshotSeed` branches appear in Feed, Explore, map/place, review detail, and Profile. Profile also ships a hidden rank-preview tool behind a long press in development.

Impact: production modules carry non-product states and conditional paths that inflate cognitive and regression load.

Recommendation: centralize screenshot fixtures behind one adapter/build flag and inject the fixture state through route-level test helpers.

Evidence: `app/(tabs)/(profile)/profile.tsx:49`, `app/(tabs)/(home)/home.tsx:89`, `components/explore/ExploreMap.tsx:181`.

#### 12. Navigation compatibility routes should have an explicit retirement policy

The app retains aliases for `/sign-in`, `/sign-up`, `/u/[username]`, `/locations/[location]`, and `/places`, plus a `places()` compatibility builder. These may be valid deep-link contracts, but there is no visible deprecation inventory.

Impact: seemingly unused features cannot be safely deleted, and route terminology continues to drift.

Recommendation: document each alias as permanent external contract or add a versioned removal date and analytics check.

Evidence: `app/sign-in.tsx`, `app/sign-up.tsx`, `app/u/[username].tsx`, `app/locations/[location].tsx`, `app/places/index.tsx`, `utils/routes.ts:210`.

#### 13. React correctness/performance lint queue — resolved

The audit originally found 21 warnings. Obsolete state paths were removed, screenshot-only effects were folded into their data completion paths, and legitimate route/database synchronization boundaries now have narrow rationale comments.

Impact: avoidable extra renders and brittle state transitions in the most interaction-heavy screens.

Result: `npm run lint` completes with zero errors and zero warnings.

## Duplicate and unused assessment

- No confidently unused customer-facing feature was found from route and import tracing.
- The strongest duplication is conceptual: legal text in onboarding versus Terms, selection-control implementations, and screen-local mutation/error-state patterns.
- Compatibility routes and screenshot-only branches are deletion candidates only after deep-link analytics and release tooling confirm they are unnecessary.
- `LocationVerifiedBadge` initially looks orphaned because it is re-exported through the shared barrel, but it is actively used across place, grid, map, and review interfaces.

## Recommended sequence

1. Fix legal document reachability and notification error feedback.
2. Simplify the Explore header and enforce 44-point targets.
3. Reduce feed-card and profile-header density using the current design system.
4. Extract the four oversized modules behind deeper interfaces.
5. Consolidate selection controls and spacing tokens.
6. Isolate screenshot scaffolding and classify legacy routes.
7. Run a fresh device audit covering Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, dark/light mode, visitor/member states, location denied, offline/error states, and small/large iPhones.
